The Federal Employment Tracking Tool provides detailed data visualizations on headcounts and full-time equivalents (FTEs) in the federal public service. It allows parliamentarians and the public to explore recent federal workforce developments and historical staffing trends across departments and agencies, comparing headcount and FTE trends over time for the government overall, by department, or by employment tenure.

Personnel costs are the largest component of federal operating spending, making the size of the federal public service a key driver of overall government expenses. This tool provides timely, accessible data to support parliamentarians’ scrutiny of the Government’s staffing and spending decisions.

The tool presents two measures of federal employment each suited to a different purpose. FTEs measure employment based on hours worked, with one FTE equivalent to one full-time position; this measure is commonly used to cost personnel expenses, since it reflects labour input. Headcount measures the number of federal employees without adjusting for hours worked; this measure is commonly used to track the number of people employed, for example in assessing organizational size.

Headcount and FTE figures in the tool are calculated by the Parliamentary Budget Office (PBO) as quarterly and annual averages, using data provided by the Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat (TBS), the Department of National Defence (DND) and the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP). These figures will differ from the federal public service headcount published annually by the government, which reflects a point-in-time count as of March 31 rather than a period average.
